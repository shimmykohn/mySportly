# mySportly

A native SwiftUI live TV app for iPhone and iPad, targeting iOS/iPadOS 27.0+ with Xcode 27.1. It turns a large Xtream/M3U catalog into an Apple TV–style Home around the teams, leagues and channels you care about.

## Open and run

Open `mySportly.xcodeproj`, choose the **mySportly** scheme, and select an iPhone or iPad running iOS 27 or later (or the iOS Simulator). Use the existing development team or your own signing team.

The first launch offers **Connect a Source** and **Explore Sample Catalog**. Enter provider credentials in the app. Sample channels and schedules are explicitly labeled; their player opens Apple’s public HLS test stream. Add the launch argument `--demo` for an isolated, in-memory sample profile during UI testing (`--demo-sports-api-check` adds a live sample game, `--demo-home-follows` follows the Knicks, adds the NBA and favorites a sample channel).

## The app

- **Four tabs: Home · My Stuff · Watch · Search**, with an adaptive iPad sidebar. The Guide opens from Home (toolbar button, or the **On Now** header). Settings opens from the profile button on Home and holds Sources, Region and Language, Appearance, iCloud sync, and OpenAI.
- **Home** is Apple TV–inspired: a hero of up to three pages (a followed team's live game, then its game later today, then the biggest live or upcoming game in an added league by matching streams, then a live program on a favorite channel), **Your Teams**, one row per added league (live and next 24 hours, without repeating Your Teams), **Your Channels**, and **On Now** (up to twelve live programs from the head of your lineup beyond your favorites; its header opens the Guide). Long-pressing a game, channel or listing offers Watch, Follow, Favorite, Hide Channel and details. Game art uses colors taken from both team logos, with the logos on Liquid Glass. Rows with nothing to show are hidden; with nothing followed, Home shows one prompt to follow teams and leagues. Home is built by `HomeContent.build` from the launch snapshot first and updates when guide matching finishes; it makes no data request or model call.
- **Player:** a full-screen screen that zooms out of the tapped game, hero or channel. Portrait shows the native AVKit player (AirPlay, PiP, audio and subtitle menus), the title, channel and league, live progress for the current program, and a favorite heart; transport controls are the system player's own. Swipe down (or the chevron) minimizes into the mini player in the tab bar; landscape on iPhone shows only the video. First-frame readiness, bounded reconnects and automatic alternate feeds on failure are unchanged.
- **Watch (movies and TV shows):** Xtream sources' movies and series in poster shelves by category (Recently Added first), a Movies | TV Shows switch, and Continue Watching. Movie pages offer Play or Resume (with time left), Play from Beginning and Mark as Watched; show pages pick the next episode, list seasons and episodes with progress and watched marks. The catalog loads the first time Watch (or a search) needs it, is sealed on the device (`vod.<source>.sealed`, never synced) and refetched after a day or on pull to refresh; details are fetched when a page opens. Progress and watched marks are personal data and sync with iCloud, written on pause, stop, end and every five minutes. Home shows the most recent unfinished title as a card at the top; Search has a Movies & TV button and Movies / TV Shows results. Movies and episodes play in the same player (resume, seek, Next Episode); Watch Two stays live-only. **Downloads:** a movie page's download button (or Download in any movie or episode's long-press menu) saves the provider's file on the device through a background URL session that keeps going when the app is closed, one at a time since providers count it as a connection; Watch shows a Downloads row, saved titles play from the device (offline too, with their poster), and long-press removes them. Downloads stay on the device and are excluded from backups; Settings → Downloads shows the space used, removes single downloads by swipe, and removes all of them. M3U sources stay live-only. See `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-16-movies-tv-design.md`.
- **Watch Two:** a second live stream under the first in portrait (side by side in landscape), one sound at a time; the mini player shows the live video. A Streams button beside what's playing switches feeds, and appears on tap in landscape and over the system full-screen player.
- **Follow model:** **Follow** a team, **Add** a league, **Favorite** a channel. My Stuff lists Teams, Leagues and Channels with swipe to remove; team and league pages show upcoming games on your lineup. Followed teams can notify you when a game starts (off by default, per team). A one-time, non-destructive migration turned older saves into follows and favorites. "Hide Channel" stays in channel context menus.
- **Titles:** every screen uses the standard (inline) navigation title; shelf headers use the Headline text style.
- **Search:** recent searches and short Liquid Glass browse buttons (Sports, News, Movies, Kids, your leagues, common sports) before typing; instant local results grouped as **Teams**, **Live Now** and **Channels** while typing. With an OpenAI key, a query local matching can’t interpret is sent to the Decisions API and the answer is searched locally, in place.
- **Guide** (pushed from Home; `mysportly://guide` opens it there): current/tonight/date guide, list and iPad timeline, program details with Watch Live. Xtream imports its own EPG when the optional XMLTV field is blank; missing guide mappings are recovered locally from unique same-source/country names, quality variants, provider mappings and local call signs.
- **Sources:** Xtream Codes and M3U URL/file import, XMLTV guide, encrypted local catalog, Keychain credentials, cancel/refresh/remove. Automatic refresh keeps a daily playlist and a six-hour guide update with retry backoff and OS-scheduled background processing.
- **Instant launch:** reopening shows the first screen from a small sealed launch snapshot (favorites, recents, the feeds of Home's games, the head of the lineup and their next 24 hours of listings, plus Home's games). The full catalog then decodes and prepares in the background, reusing saved metadata and guide-match stages when their input fingerprints match. Saved listings keep only now through +3 days. Debug builds print a `[Launch]` timeline measured from the kernel's process start. Measurements are in `docs/product/evidence/instant-launch-2026-11/baseline.md`.
- **AI with OpenAI:** with your key (Settings → OpenAI, stored only in this device’s Keychain), the app quietly runs its jobs while it is open (at launch once the catalog is ready, on return to the app, after a key is saved and after each refresh), sending only items it hasn’t asked about. **One catalog pass with GPT-6 Luna** (`gpt-6-luna`, Responses API, strict JSON schema; `CatalogPass`) sends the lineup in your regions and languages as numbered lines, about 300 per request, twelve requests at a time in one queue with the guide-game questions and the title pass, and gets them back line for line: a tidy name (only words from the original), a category from the numbered category tree (a group's channels vote; a clear majority is applied, a split waits for review, "none fits" asks for a name) and links between copies of one channel (only pairs local rules already suspect are combined, shown as one channel with alternate streams). Line numbers are mapped back on the device, so answers never carry a channel's identity, and stream addresses are never sent. It also names numbered sets (NFL 01 … NFL 16), which Browse, Search and the Guide fold into one row. Movie and show titles go through the same pass once Watch has loaded: a tidy title, a genre from a fixed list (the Watch filter's Genre menu) and links between copies of one title (other qualities and languages), which Watch shows once with "Other Versions" on its page. Each request's token use is added up into an estimated cost at Luna's published prices, shown in Settings → OpenAI and in the summary on Home. Matchup-looking guide listings the local parser can’t resolve are still matched to two teams with the Decisions API. Name, feed and game answers stay on the device (sealed `ai.sealed`), never sync, and never change playback; category placements sync like other personal choices; your renames, splits and corrections always win. Without a key, local name cleanup and exact duplicate grouping still apply. A first full pass on a ~57k-channel catalog costs under $1; later runs cost cents.
- Removed in the redesign: Live, Sports and Library tabs; recording, catch-up, reminders, spoiler settings, description translation, App Intents/Shortcuts, widgets, the Organizer and its diagnostics, Jev and Apple Intelligence.

## Integration setup

The app uses no mySportly account. CloudKit uses `iCloud.app.iska.mysportly`. Existing personal data stays local when cloud initialization fails. **Sync Personal Choices** takes effect after closing and reopening the app. Source secrets and the OpenAI key use the Keychain; provider caches are encrypted and excluded from backup.

## Verification

```sh
swift test --scratch-path /private/tmp/mysportly-core-build --disable-sandbox
```

UI and app tests run from the **mySportly** scheme on the iOS Simulator or a device, with `-parallel-testing-enabled NO`. Simulator launch timings include accessibility automation that XCUITest enables, so launch budgets are judged on devices.

The opt-in provider tests read a one-use `mySportly-provider-check.json` from the test host's temporary directory and delete that input afterward. Never put real credentials or API keys in source, launch arguments, or test fixtures; AI tests use recorded responses shaped like the documented API.
